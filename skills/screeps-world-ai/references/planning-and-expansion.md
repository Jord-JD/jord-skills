# Planning and expansion

## Choose rooms for the intended strategy

Score candidates against the colony's objective and actual map: source access, buildable space, controller supply, exits, safe routes, remote catchment, sponsor distance, hostile neighbours, and support cost. Distinguish RCL infrastructure unlocks from GCL ownership capacity and from the CPU and economy required to operate another room.

Do not equate a nearby room with a useful expansion. A cheap claim may add little territory, have poor connectivity, or depend on a fragile supply corridor. Equally, a defensible single-exit room can suit a particular strategy; use explicit tradeoffs rather than an absolute exit-count rule.

For observed neighbour strategies, compare the whole operating system: mature hubs, delivered remote income, hauling roads, reservation coverage, and available defence. Room count or a screenshot alone does not reveal why a strategy works.

## Make plans executable

Evaluate logistics travel, congestion, working positions, spawn access, defence, and construction cost alongside compactness and appearance. Check access to every serviced structure, including extension interiors and controller buffers. Use path or flood-fill checks and inspect representative layouts where geometry is consequential.

Generate candidates with explicit inputs and stable selection. Preserve the selected core or spawn position through bootstrap instead of evaluating one anchor and building at another. Version resumable searches and invalidate obsolete state without repeatedly discarding valid progress.

Use RCL-aware construction priorities that address the current bottleneck. Reserve essential working tiles so roads or secondary sites do not block source containers, links, or creep access. Pace construction toward usable infrastructure; monitor sites that remain invalid, inaccessible, or unstarted.

Keep these states distinguishable: candidate search, preview plan, accepted plan, construction, migration, and completed infrastructure. A preview or a construction request is not an operational terminal, tower, or spawn. Routine additions unlocked by RCL should not depend on completing an unrelated full-base relocation.

## Migrate without breaking the colony

Evaluate rebuilding energy, lost investment, downtime, and expected operating savings. Treat predicted savings as estimates and measure the effect after a usable phase completes. Short-window construction expenditure alone is not evidence that a layout is inefficient.

Separate relocation analysis from execution. Preserve essential spawn, storage, logistics, and defensive service throughout the transition. Normally establish replacement capacity before removing the structure it replaces; if that is impossible, make the funded recovery path and downtime explicit within the authorized plan.

Persist phase, progress, target version, and blockers so migration survives resets and deployment. Verify prerequisites immediately before destructive steps. Permit independent preparation where safe, while coordinating work that could compete for essential capacity. A migration lock should not stall ordinary development everywhere indefinitely.

## Make gates lead to progress

Record why selection, construction, or expansion is blocked, when the decision was evaluated, and what would change it. Request scouting when fresher intelligence is the missing prerequisite. Distinguish missing observations, incomplete path searches, unaffordable operations, and genuinely inaccessible candidates.

Remove or cool down failed candidates so they cannot occupy a selection slot forever. Preserve deliberate manual choices, but revalidate old candidate data before it can trigger claiming, attacking, or retirement.

Check interacting gates for circular dependencies. For example, startup cannot require income that only the missing startup crew can produce. A growth rule based on bucket trend must handle a bucket already at its cap. Budgeting must permit the useful work that an admitted project requires.

Tune these rules to the requested expansion posture. Aggressive growth can use bounded trials and earlier reinvestment; conservative growth can require stronger measured returns. Neither should silently become a permanent refusal to act. Verify the full transition from selection through travel, bootstrap, local recovery, and sustained operation.
