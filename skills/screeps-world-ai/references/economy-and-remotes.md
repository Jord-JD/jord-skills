# Economy and remotes

## Follow energy through the system

Trace source output through collection, transport, buffering, spawning, and useful spending. Full source containers with empty spawn extensions indicate a different problem from depleted sources. An idle upgrader can lack scheduling, an accessible seat, a feeder, or budget even when storage is full.

Keep separate measurements for harvesting, source collection, remote imports, internal transfers, inventory changes, and productive spending. Repeatedly moving the same energy between stores does not create income. Identify the accounting boundary and units for every KPI.

For profitability, include the operation's attributable replacement, reservation, supply, repair, and defence costs. Record cross-home support against the operation it serves. Distinguish actual expenditure from amortized upkeep estimates and avoid subtracting both as the same cost. Explain how inventory and construction investment are treated so converting energy into a creep or building does not appear as unexplained loss or free output.

Keep forecasts, startup periods, complete measured windows, and stale data distinct. If accounting changes, mark when the new cost coverage becomes representative. Compare enough ticks to include the relevant trips and replacement cycle; show uncertainty when a short window is dominated by births, combat, or construction. Net output per CPU is useful only with a stated definition of output and a comparable CPU window.

## Maintain a recoverable local economy

Check spawn requests against both capacity and energy available now. A mature room may rationally wait for a larger body, while a dead or bootstrap room needs an affordable way to restore harvesting and delivery. Avoid an unaffordable request blocking all useful recovery work.

Preserve controller safety in rooms without a completed spawn. Support workers must be able to collect, deliver, build, and upgrade according to the actual recovery bottleneck. Do not let optional signing, routine construction, or an empty-spawn assumption divert the only viable recovery worker.

Validate body cost, useful active parts, terrain speed, spawn time, and service life. Replace before coverage is lost, accounting for spawn queue and travel. Do not replace a healthy creep for a larger body unless it improves effective output or solves a measured capacity limit. Count pending replacements without treating them as already working.

Let haulers deliver a useful partial load when collection is exhausted and delivery is needed. Give logistics requests clear ownership or equivalent coordination; release stale assignments and reject inaccessible targets so one impossible delivery cannot monopolize the fleet.

Fund upgrading and construction from an explicit policy that preserves recovery and survival. Budgets should be able to buy a useful unit when a project is admitted, and rates should reflect the ticks remaining in the budget period. Stabilize focus changes with a commitment or switching rule appropriate to the project, preserving useful existing crews during transitions. Verify sustained supplied work rather than desired WORK counts alone.

## Operate remotes as complete supply chains

Evaluate a candidate with actual connectivity, route length, terrain, available source output, reservation, transport, maintenance, threat, sponsor energy, spawn time, and CPU. A source's theoretical output is not delivered income. Cap forecasts by the fleet the home can actually support.

Separate new investment from recovery of an established operation. Requiring positive measured income before allowing any startup creates a deadlock; fund a bounded trial with an explicit review condition. Conversely, old profitability does not justify restarting blindly after ownership, route, or threat changes.

Size transport per source from delivered throughput, loaded and empty travel, collection delay, and backlog. Distinguish insufficient capacity from inaccessible containers, congestion, stale assignments, and supply diverted to another job. A source link counts as transport only when it actually clears source energy.

Time miner, hauler, and reserver replacement using remaining useful service, travel, and coverage. Avoid overlapping replacement generations or retiring support before its successor reaches the working position. Treat a reservation as maintained service rather than a reason to spawn a fixed number of CLAIM creeps indefinitely.

When moving an operation between homes, reconcile active crews, pending spawn commitments, stored assignments, costs, and routes together. Keep useful coverage during handover and prevent both homes from paying for the same replacement. Reassess forecasts after route or ownership changes.

Build and repair roads on validated routes in active use. Prioritize segments by traffic, fatigue saved, build cost, and maintenance. Preserve observed elapsed trip time for fleet economics even if the geometric path has been simplified. Verify completed deliveries and net returns after the fleet settles before increasing scale on the strength of a forecast.
